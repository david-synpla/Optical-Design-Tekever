# Run 016 research delta — on-axis afocal pupils and field curvature

These sources are research guidance, not customer requirements and not proof that their demonstrated systems transfer directly to TEKEVER.

- Jessica A. Steidle, Joseph M. Howard and Jannick P. Rolland, “Constraining first-order pupil locations of rotationally symmetric imaging systems,” *JOSA A* 41, 1713–1722 (2024), [DOI 10.1364/JOSAA.528638](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAA.528638). Their first-order matrix result shows that two elements have limited pupil-location control and at least three are needed to specify pupil locations freely in all cases. Run 016 therefore does not classify an extra backend power as intrinsically wasteful, but also does not add one unless the real channel design needs it.
- Anwesh Kumar Mishra et al., “On-axis afocal telescopes as framework for Cubesat based astronomical imagers and slit-less spectrographs,” arXiv:2507.08323 (2025), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08323v1). The paper identifies the convex-secondary exit pupil as inaccessible, reports good imaging over about 20–30 arcmin, and emphasizes that final field and plate scale are set by the camera. This directly motivated testing our approximately 30 arcmin EO diagonal half-field with a perfect camera before designing real backends.
- Nishant Neeraj Gadey, “Analytical design procedure for a flat-field three-mirror afocal telescope,” *Applied Optics* 65, 4781–4790 (2026), [DOI 10.1364/AO.592131](https://doi.org/10.1364/AO.592131). The paper treats Petzval curvature as a real afocal-feeder constraint and presents a three-mirror flat-field route. Run 016 uses it only as a comparison principle; no three-mirror candidate is promoted because the two-mirror front remains exceptionally well corrected once its radial defocus is removed.

The numerical result agrees with the qualitative literature picture: A6_f1p0 is essentially perfect on axis and near the matched obscured-pupil limit after removing a field-dependent radial quadratic, but its approximately 60–63 mm focal-surface radius is too curved for the flat EO and SWIR detectors without backend correction. The literature changed the interpretation from “off-axis front-end aberration” to “field-curvature burden assigned to the channel cameras.”
