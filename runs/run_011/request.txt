## New project-level objective

The final recommendation must not optimise optical performance alone.

TEKEVER may rationally prefer a somewhat lower-performance telescope if it offers a large reduction in:

* optical procurement cost;
* non-recurring engineering;
* manufacturing complexity;
* alignment/metrology burden;
* supply-chain risk;
* production cost;
* development time.

Therefore Track B must ultimately produce a **performance-versus-cost/risk portfolio**, not simply one maximum-performance design.

At least one credible **lower-cost alternative** must be developed far enough to compare meaningfully against B05 and B06 before Track B is frozen.

Do not reject a cheaper design merely because its nominal optical metrics are worse than B05/B06. Determine instead how much performance is sacrificed and what engineering/cost advantage is gained.

## Important distinction

"COTS" is not itself the requirement.

Maximise COTS only where doing so produces a meaningful economic or development advantage.

A hybrid system using:

* a stock primary/mirror set or commercial telescope optics;
* a small custom corrector or relay;
* custom lightweight mechanics;
* a custom detector/focus interface

may be preferable to either:

1. a completely stock commercial product that does not meet the needs, or
2. a completely custom high-performance optical system.

Search explicitly for such hybrid solutions.

## Low-cost candidate selection gate

Before committing substantial optimisation effort, perform a short evidence-based comparison of the credible cheap routes.

For each candidate assess:

* expected optical hardware cost class;
* expected custom-optics content;
* number and difficulty of custom powered surfaces;
* likely NRE;
* mechanical adaptation needed;
* alignment/metrology difficulty;
* mass/volume implications;
* thermal concerns;
* detector-interface complexity;
* availability/supply risk;
* production scalability;
* available optical evidence;
* missing evidence that would require supplier data or physical testing.

Use current real COTS products and realistic procurement information where research access permits.

Record dates and sources for current products/prices. Do not treat retail price as final production cost.

Do not invent proprietary prescriptions.

If a commercial system cannot be simulated credibly without unavailable prescription data, state that explicitly and define the minimum empirical measurement needed.

## Develop at least one cheap architecture

After the selection gate, advance at least one lower-cost candidate.

Preference should be given to an architecture that can actually be engineered/modelled rather than remaining only a product brochure comparison.

For example, if justified by the evidence, B09 could use a conventional/COTS paraboloidal primary and flat secondary with a deliberately designed small corrector/extender/relay to meet the required approximately:

* 794.2 mm effective focal length;
* 128.1 mm entrance pupil;
* f/6.2;
* IMX545 active field;
* 430–800 nm band;

This is an example, not an instruction to force B09 to win.

Likewise investigate whether B01/B03 can be made materially more suitable through limited custom adaptation without destroying their cost advantage.

## Performance evaluation

Evaluate the developed cheap candidate using, as far as technically possible, the same framework used for B05/B06:

* field coverage;
* RMS spot size;
* MTF at 50/100/150/Nyquist;
* 2×2 pixel ensquared energy;
* EFL/image scale;
* physical aperture/vignetting;
* 800 m focus/refocus;
* packaging implications;
* sensitivity where practical;
* thermal issues;
* expected throughput.

Do not demand that it equal B05 or B06.

Instead quantify the performance loss.
